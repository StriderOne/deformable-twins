# Conditional spring loss sensitivity

[[E-0008]] / [[P-0006]] / [[BL-0008]], executed [[R-0024]], source posterior [[R-0022]]; informs [[H-0001]].

**Observation.** Both selected object springs were varied individually, with all remaining object and controller springs fixed to final posterior medians. Each loss is a fresh full forward calibration rollout with masked Gaussian tracking NLL, without its additive constant and without KL. This is the observation loss used in our VI, not the original upstream composite objective. No training was performed; inherited optimizer settings in the plan are unused. Training-time gradient truncation affects the source posterior, but no backward gradient is used in this probe.

**Observation.** Spring 90875 has physical posterior/prior std ratio 0.999997; spring 94845 has minimum ratio 0.203996. Posterior sampling gives NLL ranges 1.35788 and 168.80188, respectively; NLL standard deviations are 0.38467 and 27.96126. The common stiffness grid gives ranges 1.53828 and 478.28625 (about 311 times apart). Fixed-baseline NLL mean is 123056.28488, standard deviation 0.06370, observed range 0.19080. Baseline repeat range is a diagnostic, not a confidence bound on every perturbed simulation.

**Inference.** For these two selected springs, the one whose posterior contracted more has substantially greater conditional influence on tracking loss. The near-one spring has a small but discernible trend above baseline variability. This comparison does not establish a relationship across all springs and does not quantify the contribution of prior versus data globally.

**Observation / interpretation.** On the probed common grid, the strongly contracted spring favors lower stiffness; its posterior median is not an interior minimum of this frozen-coordinate loss slice. A narrow posterior therefore cannot here be read as a sharp quadratic likelihood well centered on the median. The posterior optimizes an expectation with KL and other random stiffnesses, unlike this fixed-coordinate calculation; imperfect optimization and truncated training gradients remain possible contributors.
